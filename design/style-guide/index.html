Four rules that make it Pacific Current

Calm and accountable

Navy, white and teal carry the page. Lots of room, plain layouts, prices and numbers shown plainly. The buyer is weighing risk, so nothing shouts.

Amber is the signature

Beacon amber marks the few things that matter most: live status, highlights and the Most popular badge. If everything is amber, nothing is.

Mono means the machine is talking

IBM Plex Mono is used only for what the AI technician or a system says: fix logs, ticket IDs, device names and live counts. People's words are never mono.

Two weights

Regular (400) and semibold (600), in every family. Nothing else. The browser is told not to fake a bold.

Color

The site is light only. Two components, the fix log and the footer, sit on a deep water surface. Every text pair below passes WCAG AA (4.5:1 for body text). Control borders pass the 3:1 non-text minimum. Ratios were computed from the hex values with the WCAG 2 formula.

Where amber goes

Use Beacon for

  • The Most popular badge and the featured plan's frame
  • Live status that is in progress: [n] items in progress
  • Highlights in copy: [the one phrase that matters most]
  • The human approval line in the fix log
  • The current page marker in the nav

Don't

  • Set amber text on a light background. Use --beacon-ink instead.
  • Make an amber button. Primary actions are always Current teal.
  • Use more than one amber highlight per screen of copy.
  • Use Shallows or Beacon fills behind body text on light surfaces.

Type

Three self-hosted OFL families, two weights. Source Serif 4 gives headlines an established, grown-up voice. IBM Plex Sans keeps body copy and tables plain. IBM Plex Mono is reserved for the machine.

Headings

Source Serif 4

Semibold 600 for every heading and the price. Regular 400 is allowed for long pull quotes.

Body and interface

IBM Plex Sans

Regular 400 for reading, semibold 600 for labels, buttons and emphasis.

Machine voice

IBM Plex Mono

Regular 400 only. A ticket ID [id], a device name [device-name], or a live count [n].

Rules

  • Sentence case everywhere, including buttons and headings. No all-caps labels.
  • Line height 1.6 for body, 1.25 for headings, 1.1 for the hero and page titles.
  • Prices and tables use tabular figures so columns line up.
  • Headlines balance their line breaks. Paragraphs avoid one-word last lines.
  • Fallback fonts are size-matched to the web font's x-height, so text doesn't jump when the font arrives.

Fix log: the signature element

The fix log shows the AI technician working a real ticket, line by line. It's the site's best proof, so it appears on the home page, every service page and the how-it-works page. It is always dark and always mono. The human approval line is always amber, so the reader sees where a person stepped in.

  • Markup is an ordered list, so screen readers announce each step in order.
  • Lines fade in one after another only if the reader allows motion. Otherwise they're all there at once.
  • On narrow screens the step label stacks above its line.
  • Logs on the live site must be real, anonymized tickets with permission. Never invent one and present it as real.

Voice and tone

We sound like a senior IT lead explaining things to a COO: calm, specific and on the record. The reader is weighing risk, so every sentence should make the decision feel safer.

Do

  • Say who does what: name whether the system or a person acts, and who approves.
  • Use exact numbers, and say where they came from.
  • Write for a company of 10 people and of 1,000. Avoid jargon that only one of them uses.
  • Use "you" and "your team". Use "we" for the company.
  • Put the price and the limits in plain sight.
  • Keep sentences short. One idea each.

Don't

  • Use hype words: revolutionary, seamless, cutting-edge, next-gen, AI-powered.
  • Promise the AI never makes mistakes. Say what catches them.
  • Use fear to sell security.
  • Write "contact us for pricing".
  • Put people's words in mono, or the machine's words in the serif.
  • Publish a market number without a citation.

Notes for the build

  • Copy tokens.css and site.css into the Hugo assets/css/ folder and the three font files into static/fonts/. Update the url() paths in tokens.css to match.
  • Preload only Plex Sans and Source Serif. Plex Mono can load when it's needed.
  • Components only use semantic tokens (--color-text, not --deep-water). The palette names exist so the semantic layer has something to point at.
  • Styles are in cascade layers: reset, base, layout, components, utilities. Page-specific styles go in a later layer, never with !important.
  • No framework. The only JavaScript is the form error script at the bottom of this page, which the contact form needs too.
